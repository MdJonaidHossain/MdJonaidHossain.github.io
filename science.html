Research

One question, seven systems

Every project below asks a version of the same question: what limits the movement of carbon and nitrogen inside a plant, and which molecules set that limit. The organism changed — a weed, a grass, a tomato, a microalga — and so did the method, from spectrophotometry to HPC-scale omics. The question did not.

2025 – present

Sugar transporters and CO2 acclimation in plants

Institut Jean-Pierre Bourgin (IJPB), INRAE Versailles · Université Paris-Saclay · with Dr Rozenn Le Hir

Rising atmospheric CO2 hands a plant more carbon than its metabolism is built to spend. The surplus has to go somewhere, and in a leaf cell that place is the vacuole — the store whose membrane carries SWEET2, SWEET16 and SWEET17, the transporters that fill it and drain it again. My thesis treats those three as a buffer, and asks what breaks when the buffer is removed.

Do vacuolar SWEET transporters act as the buffer that prevents carbon overflow under elevated CO2 — and where does acclimation saturate when that buffer is gone?

2024 – 2025

Aureochrome signalling in Nannochloropsis

Department of Plant Biology, Michigan State University, USA

Aureochromes are blue-light receptors unique to stramenopiles: a LOV domain that absorbs the photon and a bZIP domain that binds DNA, in that unusual order. I asked whether they set how well a microalga survives high light, cold and freezing — and whether day length changes the answer. Wild type and aureochrome mutants were grown on 8 h and 12 h photoperiods, then challenged; RNA-seq under a plain 12 h / 12 h cycle mapped the genes under aureochrome control.

Does day length change what aureochromes do — and which genes are under their control when nothing is stressing the cell?

2023 – 2024

Long-distance sugar transport and its regulators

Institute for Molecular Physiology & Institute of Synthetic Biology, Heinrich Heine University, Düsseldorf · Master thesis

Phloem loading is a bottleneck: if sucrose does not move out of the leaf, the plant cannot grow into its own photosynthesis. I drove AtSWEET11 in bundle sheath cells with the PULSE optogenetic system — transcription switched on and off with red light — to test whether loading can be re-engineered. In parallel, EMS mutagenesis with whole-genome and RNA sequencing went looking for the regulators that decide where assimilate goes.

Can phloem loading be switched on at will — and which genes decide whether the sugar ends up in roots, in seeds, or nowhere useful?

2022 – 2023

Deep learning for single-cell analysis on an HPC cluster

Gesellschaft für wissenschaftliche Datenverarbeitung mbH Göttingen (GWDG), Germany

Single-cell methods multiply faster than anyone can benchmark them. I helped put the DANCE framework — clustering, cell-type annotation, imputation, multimodal integration — onto GWDG's Scientific Compute Cluster as a service, so that a biologist can reproduce and compare models without first becoming a systems administrator. This is the project that turned me into someone who is comfortable at the scale where the data no longer fits on a laptop.

2022

Natural selection on flower size in a selfing species

Department of Plant Biology, Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences (SLU), Uppsala

A selfing plant is not supposed to need a big flower. Arabidopsis thaliana nevertheless varies in flower size across its range — and the variation is not random. Combining quantitative genetics, population genomics and niche modelling, the work showed that flowers stay large and variable at the centre of the range and shrink towards its climatic edges, where resources are tight and the trade-off against other organs bites. Published in Molecular Ecology (2026).

2022

Amino acid permeases and nitrogen partitioning in tomato

Department of Plant Production, Universitat Politècnica de València (UPV), Spain

Nitrogen reaches a tomato fruit as amino acids, and amino acids cross membranes through AAP transporters. Across four cultivars of contrasting nitrogen-use efficiency, and across leaves, roots and fruits at two nitrogen supplies, I asked which AAPs load the phloem and which unload it — because a cultivar that fruits well on less fertiliser is worth more than one that needs more.

Which AAP genes carry nitrogen into the fruit — and can they be selected for, so a crop yields the same on less fertiliser?

2021 – 2022

Stress physiology of Stipa caudata

Department of Agroforestry Ecosystems, UPV, Spain · with Universidad de Chile

A biochemical portrait of a grass under salinity and drought. Spectrophotometric profiling of proline, malondialdehyde, soluble sugars, phenolics, flavonoids, chlorophylls and ion balance, against soil-based drought models, to see which pathways the species switches on — and what it gives up to do so.
