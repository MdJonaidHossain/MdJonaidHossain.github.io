Current work

Sugar transporters and CO2 acclimation in plants

2025 – present · Institut Jean-Pierre Bourgin (IJPB), INRAE · with Dr Rozenn Le Hir

Rising atmospheric CO2 hands a plant more carbon than its metabolism is built to spend. My PhD asks where that surplus goes — and what breaks when the plant can no longer park it. The answer runs through the vacuole, and through the tonoplast SWEET transporters that fill and drain it.

Question
Do vacuolar SWEET transporters (SWEET2, SWEET16, SWEET17) act as the buffer that prevents carbon overflow under elevated CO2, and what happens to growth and metabolism when that buffer is removed?
Approach
Arabidopsis thaliana mutant lines under controlled elevated CO2, read out with RNA-seq, GC–MS and LC–MS/MS metabolomics, RT-qPCR, LI-6800F gas exchange and stable-isotope physiology — plus AlphaFold3 structures, docking and GROMACS simulation.
Why it matters
Carbon allocation is the step between photosynthesis and yield. Knowing which transporters set that allocation, and where it saturates, turns it into a breeding and engineering target rather than a description.
