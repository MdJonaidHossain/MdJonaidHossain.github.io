Current work

Sugar transport, transporters and CO2 acclimation in plants

2025 – present · Institut Jean-Pierre Bourgin (IJPB), INRAE · with Dr Rozenn Le Hir

Rising atmospheric CO2 hands a plant more carbon than its metabolism is built to spend. My PhD asks where that surplus goes — and what breaks when the plant can no longer park it. The answer runs through the vacuole, and through the tonoplast SWEET transporters that fill and drain it.

Question
Do vacuolar SWEET transporters (SWEET2, SWEET16, SWEET17) act as the buffer that prevents carbon overflow under elevated CO2, and what happens to growth and metabolism when that buffer is removed?
Approach
Arabidopsis thaliana mutant lines under controlled elevated CO2, read out with GC–MS and LC–MS metabolomics, RT-qPCR and ddPCR, LI-6800F gas exchange and stable-isotope physiology — plus AlphaFold3 structures, docking and GROMACS simulation to ask how the transporters themselves behave.
Why it matters
Carbon allocation is the step between photosynthesis and yield. Knowing which transporters set that allocation, and where it saturates, is what makes it a breeding and engineering target rather than a description.
Earlier projects

Seven institutes, one through-line

Every project below asks a version of the same question: what limits the movement of carbon and nitrogen inside a plant, and which molecules set that limit. The systems changed; the question did not.

2024 – 2025

Aureochrome signalling in Nannochloropsis

Department of Plant Biology, Michigan State University

Whether blue-light receptors set stress tolerance in a microalga. Wild-type and aureochrome mutants under 8 h and 12 h photoperiods, challenged with high light, cold and freezing; RNA-seq to map the genes under aureochrome control.

2023 – 2024

Long-distance sugar transport and transporters

Institute for Molecular Physiology & Institute of Synthetic Biology, Heinrich Heine University

Master thesis. Drove AtSWEET11 in bundle sheath cells with the PULSE optogenetic system to test whether phloem loading can be re-engineered, and ran EMS mutagenesis with whole-genome and RNA sequencing to find the regulators of assimilate partitioning.

2022 – 2023

Deep learning for single-cell analysis on HPC

GWDG, Göttingen

Deployed the DANCE benchmarking framework as a service on GWDG's Scientific Compute Cluster, so single-cell models could be reproduced and compared without each group building its own compute stack.

2022

Selection on flower size in a selfing species

Department of Plant Biology, Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences

Quantitative genetics, population genomics and niche modelling across the range of Arabidopsis thaliana. Flower size turned out to vary far more than a selfer should allow — published in Molecular Ecology.

2022

Amino acid permeases in phloem loading

Department of Plant Production, Universitat Politècnica de València

Which AAP genes load nitrogen into the phloem and unload it into fruit, across four tomato cultivars with contrasting nitrogen-use efficiency — groundwork for breeding varieties that yield on less fertiliser.

2021 – 2022

Stress physiology of Stipa caudata

Department of Agroforestry Ecosystems, UPV, with Universidad de Chile

Biochemical profiling under salinity and drought — proline, MDA, phenolics, flavonoids, chlorophylls and ion balance — against soil-based drought models, to map how the species holds on.
